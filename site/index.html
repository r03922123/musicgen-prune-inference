How it works

Why a knockout?

Text-to-music models make a different song every time. A common trick is best-of-N: generate several takes, score them, and keep the best. It works, but every take costs a full generation, so eight takes cost eight times as much.

Early pruning cuts takes that look weak partway through, so the losers are never finished. This game is that algorithm, with you as the judge: 10 s clips decide the quarter-finals, 20 s the semi-finals, and the full 30 s the final. The AI picker plays the same bracket using CLAP, a model that scores how well audio matches a text description.

What the experiments found

Where the music comes from

Every take was made in advance with MusicGen-small (30 s, eight takes per prompt) on a single GPU. Nothing is generated while you play. Prompts are 40 captions from MusicCaps plus 10 hand-written "slow-build" prompts (orchestral build-ups, post-rock crescendos) chosen because their best moments arrive late, which is exactly where judging early goes wrong. Each take's loudness is matched with one fixed gain, so louder can't win and crescendos keep their shape.

The critics

    Related work

    • Music Arena (NeurIPS 2025): live pairwise human votes between text-to-music models. Here, you compare takes from one model, with clips that grow each round.
    • Fast Best-of-N Decoding via Speculative Rejection: early pruning of unpromising generations, for language models.
    • Deezer/Ipsos survey (9,000 people, 2025): 97% couldn't tell fully AI-generated music from human music; 80% want it labelled.

    Honest limits

    • Small samples: 40 MusicCaps prompts and 10 slow-build prompts. Every number above shows its n.
    • The critics measure different things (text match vs enjoyment vs quality) and often disagree.
    • Your games stay in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.
